1. The European Compliance Landscape (2026)

Three regulatory layers affect any AI deployment in the EU:

  • GDPR (Regulation 2016/679) — personal data residency, lawful basis, data subject rights, processor obligations
  • EU AI Act (Regulation 2024/1689) — risk-classified AI system requirements; enters full enforcement August 2026
  • Schrems II / EDPB Recommendations — transfer impact assessments for any non-EU processor

For AI deployments specifically, two practical questions dominate every EU enterprise procurement:

  1. Where does my data physically reside?
  2. Who is the data controller vs processor, and under which jurisdiction?

The PAA (Private AI-Agent Appliance) architecture was specifically designed to answer these questions with auditable certainty.

3. GDPR Compliance Mapping

Article 28 (Processor)

Under a standard PAA deployment, your organisation is the data controller. The cloud LLM provider acts as processor under their published DPA. Stratronix is neither controller nor processor for customer data — we ship hardware and open-source software only. This eliminates the Schrems II transfer-imapact-assessment burden that arises when a non-EU SaaS provider is the processor.

Article 30 (Records of Processing)

Because no third party processes your data, your RoPA entries become simpler: list the PAA as an internal tool, list the cloud LLM provider as a processor under their DPA. No multi-party processor chains.

Article 32 (Security of Processing)

  • Data at rest: AES-256 full-disk encryption on the PAA
  • Data in transit: TLS 1.3 to cloud LLM endpoints
  • Access control: role-based, integrated with company SSO via OpenClaw
  • Auditability: all agent actions logged locally, exportable to SIEM

Articles 15-22 (Data Subject Rights)

All subject data lives on the device you control. Export, rectification, and erasure are direct file operations on hardware you own — no SaaS ticket, no vendor SLA, no transfer impact assessment.

7. Honest Limitations

For engineering integrity, the PAA architecture has boundaries:

  • Cloud LLM is required for inference. The PAA does not contain a large language model that can run GPT-class reasoning locally. Local compute is for routing, redaction, and small-class embedding tasks. Heavy reasoning happens in the cloud.
  • Single-site scope. One PAA serves one office / one LAN. Multi-site deployments require multiple PAAs or a cloud-bridge topology.
  • Internet required for AI features. AI inference requires network connectivity to your chosen cloud LLM provider. Without internet, the device stores data but cannot generate AI responses. (Local automation workflows without AI can still run.)
