When a private AI agent wins on cost

The arithmetic is simple: cloud AI costs seats x months x licence, and grows every year. A private AI agent appliance costs units x US$399, once, and the second user on the same device is free. Any organisation that needs AI for more than a handful of people, or runs heavy document workloads, reaches the break-even point quickly — and a two-year hardware warranty removes the refresh anxiety that comes with an annual licence.

When to stay on cloud

If you have one or two users, no sensitive data and no data-sovereignty requirement, a cloud subscription is the simpler and cheaper first step. A private appliance becomes the answer the moment data locality becomes a legal requirement, or when AI usage across the organisation is wide enough that per-seat pricing dominates.

Frequently asked questions

Is on-premise AI always cheaper?

Not always, and not only at the start. Cloud AI is cheap for a handful of users and expensive to scale, because cost follows seats and usage. A private AI agent appliance is a fixed one-time hardware cost, so it wins when many people need AI, when usage is heavy, or when the organisation must not send data out.

Does on-premise AI need an internet connection?

No. The appliance runs local inference and can work fully offline. Internet is only needed for optional updates.

What is the main risk of cloud AI for regulated industries?

Data leaves your control. Regulators such as the EU AI Act, GDPR, Indonesia's PDP Law and China's data rules often require that sensitive data stay inside the organisation or the country.

Can an on-premise appliance still use external tools?

Yes. The agent layer connects to your own CRM, ERP, mail and internal APIs over your private network; it just does not route your data through a public cloud vendor.
